The assumptions behind this result

Every estimate rests on simplifications. Here are the ones this comparison uses, with your numbers filled in.

How this works

No black box. This is the whole method, in everyday language.

It plays out both options month by month

Instead of one big formula, ClearLedger walks through every month. Each month, interest is added to anything you owe and to anything you have saved, and then that month's money moves according to the option. Doing it step by step means the numbers you see in the table are exactly the numbers behind the chart.

Yearly rates become monthly rates

A 36% yearly rate is charged as 3% a month (36 ÷ 12). Savings work the same way. If you enter a tax rate, the savings rate is reduced by that share first: 7% with 20% tax becomes 5.6% a year.

monthly rate = yearly rate ÷ 12

Debt first or savings first

Both options spend the same money each month. Debt first sends all of it to the debt until it is gone, then everything goes to savings. Save first pays only the minimum and saves the rest. We compare your net position: what you have saved minus what you still owe.

Pay in full or pay in instalments

Both options start with the item's price set aside. Pay in full spends it today, minus any discount. Instalments pay a fee today and a fixed amount each month, while the rest keeps earning interest. The fixed monthly amount uses the standard loan formula that banks use for an EMI:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P is the price, r the monthly rate, n the number of months.

Finding the tipping point

To find where the winner changes, ClearLedger tries a value halfway through a range, checks which option wins, and keeps halving the range towards the switch. It is the same idea as guessing a number between 1 and 100 by always asking "higher or lower?". Sixty rounds of this pin the tipping point down precisely.

What it leaves out

Rates that change, late fees, inflation, emergencies, credit score effects, and the peace of mind of having cash on hand are not modelled. That is why the result is an estimate for learning, not a recommendation. For a real decision, talk to a qualified adviser.
